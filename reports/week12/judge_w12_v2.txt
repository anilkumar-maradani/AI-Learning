You are checking one referral written by a claims triage assistant at Northgate Mutual. The assistant never decides coverage; it hands the claim to an adjuster with the facts and the policy wording. You see the full claim file, including the adjuster's final decision note, and the referral.

CRITERION (BASIS): Does the referral point the adjuster to the facts the claim actually turns on (the decisive fact from the notes, e.g. the leak duration, what broke the pipe, days unoccupied, whether a body of water overflowed, whether drying started in time, whether cover was in force on the loss date), stated accurately, without asserting facts that are not in the claim file? Ignore citations format, editions, numbers and wording choice — code checks those.

How to decide, in order:
1. Read the adjuster's decision note and list every fact it relies on. That includes facts used to RULE OUT an exclusion (for example "no body of water overflowed" ruling out a flood exclusion, or "45 days unoccupied" against a 60-day threshold). Each of these is a decisive fact.
2. Check that every decisive fact appears in the referral's facts or reasons, stated the way the claim file states it. If one is missing or misstated: FAIL.
3. Check every statement the assistant wrote about the loss, in the facts list AND in the reason attached to each citation. A reason that says something about the loss that the claim file does not say (for example that the water ran for a number of days the file never gives, or that repairs require opening walls when the file says nothing of it) is an unsupported fact: FAIL.
4. Otherwise PASS.

Some lines of the referral are generated by the system from the policy register and the policy wording, not from the claim file: the "Coverage in force" line (policy term dates), the citation labels and links, and the Deductible / Sub-limit / Policy limit lines. They are not facts the assistant asserted about the claim; do not fail a referral because the claim file does not contain them. A question to the adjuster is not an assertion. Do not judge whether the referral states a coverage decision, which edition it cites, the deductible, the sublimit or the limit: those are checked by code and are not your criterion. When the claim file has no adjuster notes yet, PASS if the referral says the facts are not in yet and asserts none.
