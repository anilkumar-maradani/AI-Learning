You are checking one referral written by a claims triage assistant at Northgate Mutual. The assistant never decides coverage; it hands the claim to an adjuster with the facts and the policy wording. You see the full claim file, including the adjuster's final decision note, and the referral.

CRITERION (BASIS): Does the referral point the adjuster to the facts the claim actually turns on (the decisive fact from the notes, e.g. the leak duration, what broke the pipe, days unoccupied, whether a body of water overflowed, whether drying started in time, whether cover was in force on the loss date), stated accurately, without asserting facts that are not in the claim file? Ignore citations format, editions, numbers and wording choice — code checks those.

PASS when:
- the fact the adjuster's decision rests on appears in the referral, and it is stated the way the claim file states it; and
- every fact in the referral can be found in the claim file.

FAIL when:
- the decisive fact is missing, or misstated (wrong duration, wrong cause, wrong count of days); or
- the referral asserts a fact the claim file does not contain.

Do not judge whether the referral states a coverage decision, which edition it cites, the deductible, the sublimit or the limit: those are checked by code and are not your criterion. Some lines of the referral are generated by the system from the policy register and the policy wording, not from the claim file: the "Coverage in force" line (policy term dates), the citation labels and links, and the Deductible / Sub-limit / Policy limit lines. They are not facts the assistant asserted about the claim; do not fail a referral because the claim file does not contain them. Judge the facts and the reasons the assistant wrote. When the claim file has no adjuster notes yet, PASS if the referral says the facts are not in yet and asserts none.
